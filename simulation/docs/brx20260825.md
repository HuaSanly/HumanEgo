# BRX 20260825 right-arm scene

The entry point is `simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml`. It inherits
`pick_cube.yaml` using the existing `base_config` loader. The trained checkpoint,
camera intrinsics/extrinsics, object dimensions, perception, control timing,
grasp assistance, automatic lift and release assistance remain inherited.

## Build the local model

From the repository root, in an environment with `simulation/requirements.txt`:

```bash
python simulation/scripts/import_brx.py --source /home/huasan/BRX20260825
```

The source is a SolidWorks URDF export with `urdf/BRX20260825.urdf` and `meshes/`.
The importer validates the expected joints and referenced resources, copies the
meshes, and writes `simulation/humanego_sim/assets/robots/brx20260825/brx.xml`
and `provenance.json`. These local build assets are ignored, following the
optional Menagerie robots. The provenance records source URDF/mesh SHA-256
hashes, fixed torso angles and the MuJoCo version. It does not modify the source.
Regenerate assets after editing the importer. `--output` supports a different
build directory for inspection; the runtime uses the default directory.

## Assembly and coordinate conventions

- One whole robot is attached under `right/`; the inactive left arm remains visible.
- Only `ArmR02_Joint` through `ArmR08_Joint` and the two right jaw joints remain
  movable. Base, wheels, head, left arm and torso joints are fixed in the model.
- The two folding joints and `Trunk_Joint` are fixed at zero: the torso remains
  vertical in its original CAD pose. `Head02_Joint=0.65` and `Head03_Joint=0.95`
  radians pan the head right and tilt it down towards the table. The other
  inactive joints retain zero poses. Head pose is fixed, not actively driven.
- `rig.base_pose.right` supplies the whole robot's world position and yaw.
  The robot stays at the first BRX scene's position `[-0.14639, 0.32001, -0.32104]`,
  with zero roll, pitch and yaw, facing world +X. The floor stays at z=-0.76 m. The base
  z=-0.32104 m places the lowest wheels approximately on that floor.
- `rig.table.height=0.86` sets the floor-to-tabletop height: the tabletop is
  z=0.10 m, 10 cm above the original scene. The floor and robot do not move.
  Object construction, Python resets, sampling and browser resets all use this
  same surface height. Perceived grasp centres and table-plane keypoint filtering
  also use this height. Configs without `height` keep the original 0.76 m table.
  Adjustable height currently supports pick_cube; other tasks retain their table.
- The rear tabletop edge is x=0.19 m. The cube is at `[0.34, -0.095, 0]` and
  the container at `[0.44, 0.27, 0]` (x, y, yaw degrees). Their centres are about
  38 cm apart, versus 19 cm in the previous layout. Prop dimensions and
  physical properties remain inherited. These objects and the table adapt to the
  upright robot, rather than translating, rotating or leaning the robot.
  `rig.arm_mount=false` omits the old arm rail/brackets while retaining the
  external policy camera and its stand. `rig.view_cameras` changes only video
  framing, independently of the policy camera.
- The world/base/camera transforms come from the same effective base pose, so
  IK, observations, recordings and web exports share the same calibration.
- The TCP lies at the centre of the opposed distal pads, 73 mm along their
  local finger axis from the closed-jaw origins. Its +Z approaches along the
  fingers and +X is the finger-opening axis, matching the existing HumanEgo
  end-effector interface. No policy action reinterpretation is required.
- The existing safety floor is base-relative. BRX uses `safe_z_min=0.40 m`,
  approximately 21 mm below the raised tabletop; task grasp targets remain above it.

## Gripper and physics assumptions

At q=0 both right jaws are open. Closing drives `JawBlock01_Joint` to +0.041 m;
an equality constraint drives `JawBlock02_Joint` to the opposite displacement.
One actuator controls this pair, and the existing measured opening API reports
1=open, 0=closed. The empty opening is about 82 mm between the pad surfaces.

The URDF's zero effort/velocity fields do not specify useful motor parameters.
The importer supplies finite position servo gains, torque/force limits, joint
damping and armature for simulation. Runtime gravity compensation follows the
existing robot adapters. These are simulation settings, not measured BRX motor
or contact specifications.

Original CAD meshes provide the visuals. Arm-axis capsules and two distal
20 x 24 x 8 mm pad boxes provide the main moving contacts. The open trunk frame
and wrist camera brackets use separate convex hulls for their disconnected CAD
parts, avoiding a hull across empty space. Adjacent assembly bodies and the two
close wrist housing pairs are excluded from self-collision; other self-collision,
table/object contact and friction remain active. No grasp weld or object teleport
is used during grasping. The built-in centre eye also carries a MuJoCo camera
named `right/head_view`: its optical centre is 3.5 mm in front of the CAD lens,
and its optical axis follows the head. The 70-degree vertical field of view is
a simulation preview setting; the URDF has no factory camera calibration. The
complete cube and container, including handles, fit in this view with a margin.
The policy still uses its existing external D405 calibration and inputs.

## Validation and exports

```bash
python -m pytest simulation/tests/test_brx_scene.py -q

# Scene-only preview, using the existing CLI.
python simulation/scripts/run_episode.py --config simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml \
  --set scene_only=true --duration 5 --video --out brx_preview

# Record the actual head-mounted camera view.
python simulation/scripts/run_episode.py --config simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml \
  --set scene_only=true --duration 5 --camera_view right/head_view --video --out brx_head_preview

# Full closed-loop inference and video.
python simulation/scripts/run_episode.py --config simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml \
  --seed 0 --video --out brx_pick_cube

# Build a local browser physics preview, or a recorded policy replay.
python simulation/scripts/build_web.py --config simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml --set scene_only=true
python simulation/scripts/build_web.py --config simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml
python simulation/web/tools/serve.py 8791 simulation/web

# Freeze randomized trials using the wider BRX sampling envelope selected by its config.
python simulation/scripts/evaluate.py --config simulation/configs/pick_cube_brx.yaml \
  --n 10 --policy-seeds 0 --prepare-only --out brx_layouts
```

Select BRX under the pick_cube task in the local viewer. As with the original
pick_cube scene, policy inference is recorded in Python; browser ONNX inference
is not enabled for this task. Scene-only export supports live browser physics.

The dedicated tests cover inherited policy/camera and prop geometry, world/base
transforms, rejection of bimanual duplication, fixed-body assembly, five seconds
of stable physics, IK position/orientation, Cartesian servo tracking, measured
jaw direction/coupling, and a physical two-pad lift and release into the container.
They also enforce the original base position, zero base rotation, upright torso,
fixed floor and consistent raised-table height in sampling and browser exports.
The head-view test checks the camera's physical attachment, downward optical
axis and projection of both full props into the image. Evaluation automatically
uses `eval_pick_cube_brx.yaml` through `evaluation.sampling`; an explicit
`--sampling` still takes priority. The original task's sampling config is unchanged.
The physical grasp test uses scripted ground-truth targets to validate the mechanism;
it does not establish policy success rate. Model-dependent tests skip with an
import instruction when local BRX assets are absent.

The fixed, forward-facing upright scene passes scripted physical two-pad grasp,
lift, transport and release checks. In the wider layout's seed-0 run of the inherited
trained anchor-frame checkpoint, the policy grasped and lifted the cube, but
kept it above the container without releasing before the 40-second limit.
The cube reached z=0.3462 m; at timeout its height was z=0.2955 m and its
horizontal distance to the container centre was 0.0856 m. The new object
arrangement therefore still needs policy adaptation and broader evaluation;
past success results from the leaning or rotated robot
do not apply to this fixed pose. This is not evidence of real-hardware transfer.
The old Trossen/Franka/UR5e calibration paths remain the
defaults; BRX requires its dedicated scene config.

The previous complete non-`sim` regression produced 173 passed, 6 skipped and two
existing failures. The 14 current BRX tests passed, including the head camera and raised/lowered-table
perception checks. Browser reset was also checked with legacy metadata and
table offsets 0, +0.10 and -0.10 m.
`test_config_profiles.py::test_defaults_are_the_real_rig`
expects `look_ahead_offset=45`, while the original config contains 35;
`test_pick_cube_scene.py::test_default_pick_cube_is_standalone_without_bread_settings`
expects cube y=-0.2, while the original config contains -0.3. Those original
configs and assertions are unchanged by this scene addition.
